## What does the law ask?

For high-risk AI, the [AI Act](/en/insights/ai-act) sets two requirements that end up in the design of a system:

- **Human oversight (Article 14).** The system must be built so that people can understand it, monitor it and step in where needed. They must be able to disregard or reverse an outcome, and to stop the system. Organisations that use the system appoint people with the knowledge, authority and time to do this (Article 26).
- **Logging (Article 12).** The system must automatically record what it does, so you can trace afterwards what happened. Deployers keep those logs for at least six months.

For the high-risk applications in Annex III these requirements apply from 2 December 2027; for AI in regulated products from 2 August 2028. Those dates were set in 2026 by the Digital Omnibus. We check them every quarter.

## What that means in a system

Human oversight is a design choice. These are the patterns we use most:

1. **A person approves.** The system makes a proposal; a member of staff sends it or decides. Suits anything that goes out or affects a person.
2. **Hand over when in doubt or urgent.** The system handles standard cases and passes difficult, urgent or sensitive cases straight to a member of staff.
3. **A confidence threshold.** Below a set confidence level the case goes to a person. You adjust the threshold based on the logs.
4. **An emergency stop.** One button that halts the automated processing, while the manual work carries on.
5. **An explanation with each outcome.** The system shows what an outcome is based on, so the person checking it can see whether it is right.

## What to log, and for how long

A good log answers four questions afterwards:

| Question | What you record |
|---|---|
| What went in? | The input, or a reference to it |
| What came out? | The outcome and, where possible, the confidence or the sources |
| What happened to it? | Approved, edited, handed over or overridden, and by whom |
| When and with which version? | Time, version of the system and of the model |

For high-risk AI you keep the logs for at least six months. If they contain personal data, the GDPR also applies: no longer than necessary, and well secured. Read more in [AI and the GDPR](/en/insights/ai-and-gdpr).

## An example from practice: the Medux AI voice

For Medux we built an AI voice that handles phone calls: answering, booking appointments and responding to questions. The figures: 4,000 calls a month, 80% handled by the AI, difficult or urgent questions passed straight to a member of staff.

This is what human oversight looks like in practice: the AI for the standard work, a person where it matters, and every call transcribed for quality and review. Read [the case](/en/cases/medux).

## Adding it later or building it in from the start

Adding oversight and logging afterwards is expensive. You have to find decision points in a system that was not designed for them, and reconstruct logs that do not exist. If you include it from the start, it becomes part of the application, and you can adjust it later based on what the logs show.
